The engineer views, three ways: phone and tablet

What an engineer captures on site is what the office and the two SharePoint registers run on. Section 0 audits what each report kind has to carry, read off the office desk and the Variation Register built today. Concepts 1–3 are three ways of asking for it. Nothing here is wired to the app.

Read against
The office desk as built (Inbox, Issues, Variations, the report reader), the Issue Tracker and Variation Register lists, and EngineerWorkspace, ObservationEditor, IndividualDefect and the report page as they stand.
Devices
Phone at 390 × 812 and tablet at 1024 × 700 landscape. Below 900 CSS pixels is the phone layout, above it the tablet; rotation only changes width, never state, as today.
What does not change
Status never depends on colour alone; anything tapped is 48px; a device save is never shown as a Leodis save; drafts stay private until sent; a defect needs a photograph; the office's words (received, awaiting review, PM notified, read) are reused unchanged.
Status
Drafts, 10 September 2026. Pick one (or parts of several) and it gets built against the existing routes. The field additions in section 0 are the only data changes any concept needs.

0 · What the office extracts from each kind of update, and what it cannot get today

Three things leave an engineer's phone: a progress update, an issue (a defect or an access restriction, raised at submission or flagged individually) and a variation request. The office reads each of them in different places, and the gaps below are where the desk shows “not set”, “trade not recorded” or has to type what the engineer already knew.

What the engineer sees come back is the other half: each concept below also shows the office's state on the things the engineer raised, in the office's own words.

Concept 1

One thing at a time

The site update becomes a deck of cards: pick the kind first, answer only that kind's questions, add the next card. Home is four big actions and what came back from the office. The tablet is the same deck with the cards listed down the left, which is roughly the section list the editor has now, tidied.

What it answers first
  • “What kind of thing is this?” Asked once per card, then only that kind's questions. A progress card is three taps; a variation card is the register's six questions in site words.
  • Home leads with what came back: instructed, assigned, read, in the office's own tags.
  • Check & send says what sending does (raises ISS-, raises VO-, who gets emailed), not just what is missing.
What it assumes
  • The five optional fields from section 0 on an observation. Nothing else in the store changes.
  • Location chips from this project's previous entries (FE-1), free text always allowed (EXP-4).
  • Home's “came back” list is the engineer's own reports, issues and variations filtered from endpoints that already exist.
Build cost from today
  • Low–medium. The editor already keeps one section visible at a time; this is the kind picker moved in front of the fields and the section list restyled.
  • The variation card is the new work: one component, five fields, all optional.
  • Home is a re-layout of EngineerWorkspace with one new list.

What it answers first
  • “What did I do today, and what happened to what I raised?” One surface for both. The diary is the living site diary (EXP-1) written once, on site, not assembled afterwards.
  • The office's decisions come back onto the entry that caused them, in the office's words: instructed, assigned, verify, read.
  • Sign-off is the report. The document is generated from the day; the engineer never fills in “a report”.
What it assumes
  • Same five fields as Concept 1, plus a time on each observation (the store has capturedAt on photos; the entry needs its own).
  • “Earlier on this job” is the engineer's submitted reports joined to the issues and variations they raised — three existing endpoints, one join in the client.
  • Adding to a day after sending is the existing correction flow, presented as “rev 2”.
Build cost from today
  • Medium. The editor is turned inside out: the list is the screen and the form is a sheet. The autosave, recovery, findings and signature code is unchanged underneath.
  • The entry-after-sending page is new: report observation plus the raised record's history.
  • The tablet is the phone with the sheet docked to the right.
Concept 3

Walk by location

Engineers walk a building, not a form. Say where you are, log what is there (any kind, as many as needed), move on. The report is grouped by location and the summary at the end shows per-area counts, which is also how a project manager reads it. On the tablet the areas run down the left, the entries for the chosen area in the middle, and the running summary on the right.

What it answers first
  • “Where am I, and what's here?” The walk order is the capture order. Open issues and variations for the area sit under the new entries, so a repeat sighting is one tap, not a dropdown of refs.
  • The summary is per area, which is how the PDF and the project manager read it.
  • The variation asks its questions in site words (“Why is it extra?”) and stores the register's choices.
What it assumes
  • Areas are the distinct locations already typed on this project, counted from previous reports. No hierarchy, no naming rule (EXP-4).
  • Issues and variations have a location string to group by. They do.
  • Same five optional fields as the other two; the site-words chips are labels over the same stored values.
Build cost from today
  • Medium. Grouping observations by location is a client-side sort; the area rail is new; the section editor is reused per entry.
  • “Still open here” is the project's open issues and variations filtered by location match, loose (contains), never exact.
  • The three-column tablet is the most layout work of the three.

My recommendation: build Concept 1, and take two things from the others

Concept 1 is the capture spine. It is the shortest path from the current editor to something that is not gross: the section list becomes cards, the kind is chosen before the fields appear, and the variation card asks the six questions the register actually needs, in the order an engineer would answer them. Everything else in the editor (autosave, device recovery, findings, signature, corrections) is untouched underneath it, which is why it is the cheapest of the three and the one I would put on a phone first.

Take “what came back” from Concept 2. The engineer's home should lead with the office's answers to the things they raised, in the office's tags: instructed, assigned, verify, read, PM notified. The desk already produces every one of those words; the engineer side only has to show them. The entry-after-sending page (the office's answer at the top, history below, add a note) is worth building as the engineer's view of an issue or a variation, replacing the current issue page's layout.

Take the area chips and “still open here” from Concept 3. Location suggestions drawn from this project's previous entries, and the open issues and variations for the chosen area shown as pictures with descriptions, are what make a repeat sighting one tap. Both are FE-1 and FE-5 as already agreed, and neither needs a location catalogue. I would not build the area rail or the per-area report: the PDF groups by update, and changing that is a document decision, not a phone one.

The data change is the same whichever is chosen: five optional fields on an observation, listed in section 0. The office side built today already has somewhere to put each of them (affected trade on the issue; reason, already done, who asked, and a rough size on the variation), so the phone can start sending them the day the fields exist. I would ship the variation card and the “came back” list first, because they close the loop the Variations tab now opens, and the defect card's trade field second.

All three are static mockups. Nothing here is wired to the application. Projects, references, engineers, dates, counts and office states are examples in the Leodis format, chosen to show how the screens behave. The site-words chips in Concept 3 map one to one onto the Variation Register's Reason choices and would be stored as those. The signature, autosave and offline rules shown are the app's existing rules, drawn where they would appear; they are not new behaviour.
