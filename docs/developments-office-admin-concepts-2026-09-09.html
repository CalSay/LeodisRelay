The Developments office, three ways

Three answers to the same brief: rebuild the Office and Admin screens for Leodis Developments at the standard of the Compliance office, with a simplified version for the phone. Same palette, same typefaces, same tags and references. What changes is the structure underneath and the chrome around it.

Kept from Compliance
The 56px top bar with search and Attention. Mono tab strip with counts. Hairline panels on a recessed ground, 2px radius, sunken details column. Status as a word, a shape and a colour.
Accent roles
Brass does what aquamarine does in Compliance: the company mark, references and active state. It also stays on the one filled action per screen. Alert, caution and ok are the application’s own and are never the accent.
Data on these screens
Reports (received, review, delivery), issues (work status and confirmation as two facts), projects from the list, and the team roster with its Microsoft role. Draft contents stay private: a draft shows as a summary only.
Admin, everywhere
What Admin has today: the assignment checklist, signed-in accounts, older drafts to assign, a link to Microsoft Entra. Delivery, review and project settings are drawn as planned and tagged so.

Concept 1

Portfolio desk

The Compliance office, transposed. One top bar with search and an Attention bell; a mono tab strip with counts; and, under Projects, a column browser: Projects › Register › Details, with the details column recessed. The register column holds a project’s reports and issues together, because on site they are one stream. Admin is the last tab, shown only to Admins, and /admin lands on it. Everything on this page already exists as a primitive in compliance.css, so the two workspaces would share one component set with the accent swapped.

Admin · from Office home
What it answers first
  • “Where do we stand on each job?” The project column with its bar is the home; the details column is the project sheet until you pick a row.
  • The Inbox and Issues tabs are the flat registers for when the question is “what came in?” or “what is late?” regardless of project.
  • Admin is a tab, not a separate site: same chrome, same search, same Attention bell.
What it assumes
  • The issue bar needs four counts per project: closed, open, awaiting verification, overdue. All exist today.
  • “Not emailed” is a delivery state styled at its severity, not a timestamp.
  • Acknowledgement (FE-4) as an explicit office action, separate from receipt and delivery.
  • On the phone the browser collapses to lists; the reader is a bottom sheet; Admin is a page off Home.
Build cost from today
  • Low–medium. The primitives are already in compliance.css; scoping them to a shared stylesheet with an accent variable is the first step.
  • The Office page already fetches every report and issue; this is a re-layout of the same data plus per-project roll-ups.
  • Admin gains a session table with last sign-in, which the session store can answer today.
Concept 2

Document ledger

Construction runs on document control, so the office is drawn as one: a sheet with a title block, a dated ledger of everything that arrived or changed, and a reader that slides in from the right when a row is selected. Reports and issues sit in one ledger, typed in the first column, grouped by day. The rail on the left is the filing structure, not a menu: projects with their counts, and under each the states that need someone. Admin is a schedule of accounts on the same kind of sheet, with a revision history down the side, because that is what an access list is.

Build cost from today

Admin · settings
What it answers first
  • “What do I do this morning?” Three lanes, every card carrying its verb. Nothing on the board is a link with no action.
  • The project strip is the filter, so the same board answers “and just Kirkstall?” with one click.
  • Admin is settings: sections on the left, controls on the right, facts where Microsoft owns the answer.
What it assumes
  • A “needs a decision” rule: verify, assign, triage, and (when review is on) approve. All are existing commands.
  • The olive rail in light mode is a departure for the office; the engineer band already uses it, so it reads as Developments, not as a new brand.
  • Two settings that do not exist yet are drawn as toggles: an office copy of every email, and review per project. Both are tagged where they appear.
Build cost from today
  • Medium. The lanes are three filtered views of data the Office page already holds. The rail is new chrome.
  • Per-project figures are the same four counts as Concept 1.
  • Settings needs a small Admin API for the two toggles, or they ship as facts until it exists.

Choosing between them

ConceptAnswers firstBest whenMobileBuild cost from today
1 · Portfolio desk“Where do we stand on each job?” then “what came in?”The office thinks in projects and wants one workspace that looks and behaves like Compliance.Collapses cleanly: lists, one tab bar, the reader as a sheet.Low–medium. The primitives exist; this is layout over data already loaded.
2 · Document ledger“What happened, and when?”Most office time is reading and filing, and the record has to look like a record.A day-grouped list and a full-page reader. No browser to collapse.Medium. Merge two lists, sheet chrome, the audit surfaced for Admin.
3 · Day board“What do I do this morning?”One or two people triage across every project and want the verbs in front of them.Counters and one lane at a time; the strip scrolls sideways.Medium. Three filtered views plus new rail chrome and a small settings API.

My recommendation: build Concept 1, and take two things from the others

Concept 1 is the workspace. It is what was asked for, and it has a structural advantage the other two do not: it shares its component set with the Compliance office. One scoped stylesheet with an accent variable — brass here, aquamarine there — and the two companies stop drifting apart. Projects › Register › Details is also the right spine for Developments: the office already navigates by project, and the register column puts reports and issues in one stream, which is how site actually produces them.

Take the reader from Concept 2. Actions and decisions first, then the updates, then the signature. That ordering is the issued document’s, and it belongs in Concept 1’s details column and in the phone sheet exactly as drawn there. The title block is worth borrowing for the project sheet too; it says what a project is in one row instead of a paragraph.

Take the lanes from Concept 3. The Attention pop in Concept 1 should be the three lanes with their verbs, not a list of links, and the phone’s Home should be the same three counters over the same three lists. The olive rail I would hold: it is a good room, but it makes the Developments office look unlike the Compliance one, and sharing the chrome is worth more right now than a second identity.

Order of work: a shared workspace stylesheet lifted from compliance.css; the Projects browser with the project sheet; the Inbox with the reader; Issues; Admin as a tab with the session table and older drafts; then the phone layouts, which are the same components under a 700px breakpoint. Acknowledgement and a Team tab are drawn but not built, and should be marked as planned until they are.

All three are static mockups. Nothing here is wired to the application. Projects, references, engineers, dates, counts and delivery states are examples in the Leodis format, chosen to show how the screens behave. The roster names are the assignment checklist as it stands; last sign-in times are invented. The pilot facts on the Admin screens (a local outbox rather than email, a backup on 8 September, no SharePoint filing yet) are as recorded in the deployment handoff and should be read as illustrative, not as a live status.
